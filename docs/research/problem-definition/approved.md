# LearnAI — Graduation Project 1

## Problem Definition — Consolidated Approved Version

### Component 1: Ideal Situation

In an ideal educational environment, students engaging with digital learning materials should be able to understand the underlying concepts and apply their knowledge to meaningful problems and new situations.

Learning experiences should therefore provide accurate, learning-objective-aligned content, opportunities for active application, and constructive feedback, allowing understanding and application to be demonstrated through measurable learning outcomes.

---

### Component 1 — Approved Virtual Laboratory Extension

**LearnAI — Scientific Ideal: Virtual Laboratory Extension**

**Domain-Specific Ideal Situation**

In laboratory-based education, students should have opportunities to develop a conceptual understanding of scientific principles and apply their knowledge through active experimentation.

An ideal complementary virtual laboratory experience would enable students to interact with simulated equipment, perform scientifically accurate experimental procedures, make decisions, explore the consequences of their actions, and receive meaningful feedback.

Such experiences should provide accessible, safe, and repeatable opportunities to practice and reinforce laboratory learning, particularly when additional preparation or physical laboratory access is needed.

Virtual experimentation should support the development of understanding and procedural reasoning without being assumed to replace all practical skills acquired through physical laboratory work.

**Primary Learning Outcomes**

**Understanding:** Explain the scientific principles, observations, and reasoning behind experimental procedures.

**Application:** Apply knowledge by selecting appropriate actions, executing simulated procedures, interpreting outcomes, and correcting mistakes.

**Supporting Educational Conditions**

- Scientifically accurate representations of procedures and outcomes.
- Meaningful interaction with laboratory equipment and experimental variables.
- Opportunities for safe exploration and repetition.
- Constructive feedback connected to learning objectives.
- Accessible interaction options for diverse learners.
- Assessment of understanding and application.

**Research Boundary**

This addendum defines desirable educational experiences and outcomes.

The automatic generation of laboratories from PDFs, 3D implementation, game mechanics, avatar customization, multiplayer functionality and model selection remain aspects of LearnAI's product vision and proposed technical implementation.

Their effectiveness and feasibility require separate investigation and validation.

### Component 2: Current Reality

Physical laboratory education provides students with opportunities to apply theoretical knowledge and develop practical skills. However, laboratory-based teaching may be constrained by equipment availability, infrastructure, scheduling requirements, safety considerations, and accessibility barriers.

Virtual laboratories have emerged as complementary educational tools, enabling students to perform simulated experiments, interact with virtual equipment, repeat procedures, and receive instructional feedback. Existing platforms demonstrate that immersive 3D experimentation and interactive laboratory learning are already technically achievable.

In parallel, AI-powered educational platforms can transform uploaded learning materials into study resources, assessments, and interactive games. Advances in generative AI have also enabled research into automated game development, virtual environments, and the generation of experimental procedures.

Nevertheless, automatically generated scientific procedures can contain inaccuracies, missing actions, or incorrect sequences. Consequently, scientific validation remains an important consideration when translating educational instructions into executable simulations.

Together, these developments establish a technological landscape in which physical laboratories, interactive virtual simulations, document-based educational AI, and generative game technologies coexist.

**Research boundary:** This section describes existing educational conditions and technological capabilities. It does not establish a research gap or claim that existing systems are incapable of generating scientifically accurate, document-based virtual laboratories.

### Component 3: Specific Research Gap

**Provisional Research Gap**

Recent advances in generative AI have enabled the automated creation of interactive scientific demonstrations, experimental procedures, circuit designs, and virtual simulations. Existing digital electronics simulators also provide established environments for constructing and testing logic circuits.

However, the reliable transformation of instructor-provided digital electronics laboratory PDFs—including procedural instructions, circuit diagrams, and learning objectives—into executable educational simulations remains an area requiring further investigation.

In particular, the research must examine how generated experimental activities can preserve traceability to their source documents while also satisfying independently verifiable circuit logic and procedural constraints.

Furthermore, the educational effectiveness of such generated and validated activities should be evaluated in terms of students' conceptual understanding and ability to apply their knowledge.

**Proposed Research Focus**

LearnAI will investigate a source-grounded PDF-to-virtual-laboratory generation process for introductory digital electronics exercises.

The technical investigation will focus on:

- Accurate extraction and representation of laboratory procedures.
- Preservation of traceability between generated content and source documents.
- Independent validation of circuit behavior and experimental interactions.
- Detection of unsupported or scientifically inconsistent generated content.

The educational investigation will examine understanding and application using suitable learning assessments.

**Important Limitation**

This statement is a working research-gap hypothesis. It does not establish that no existing system addresses these requirements, nor does it claim that LearnAI has already demonstrated improved scientific accuracy or student learning.

### Component 4: Consequences

When AI-generated educational simulations do not accurately preserve the procedures, components, and learning objectives described in their source materials, students may encounter experimental activities that differ from those originally intended by their instructors.

Furthermore, simulations that appear interactive and technically functional may still produce scientifically incorrect outcomes. In digital electronics education, such inaccuracies could introduce or reinforce misconceptions about circuit behavior, experimental procedures, and the application of theoretical concepts.

For educators, the absence of reliable validation may increase the need for manual inspection, correction, and testing of generated laboratory activities, potentially limiting the practical benefits of automated content generation.

Finally, without appropriate educational evaluation, successful execution of a virtual experiment cannot by itself demonstrate that students have developed conceptual understanding or the ability to apply their knowledge to new problems.

Together, these potential consequences highlight the importance of examining source fidelity, functional fidelity, and educational outcomes as distinct aspects of AI-generated laboratory learning.

**Research limitation:** These are evidence-informed potential consequences. LearnAI has not yet conducted experiments establishing their frequency, severity, or impact on its intended student population.

---

### Component 5: Desired Outcome

The desired outcome is an educational environment in which students can access interactive virtual laboratory experiences that accurately represent the scientific concepts, experimental procedures, and learning objectives described in their instructional materials.

Within the initial domain of digital electronics education, the proposed approach aims to support the transformation of instructor-provided laboratory PDFs into structured, playable experimental activities.

These generated activities should preserve traceable relationships with their source documents while satisfying independently verifiable scientific and functional requirements, including correct circuit connections, logical behavior, and experimental outcomes. Ambiguous, incomplete, or inconsistent instructions should be identified rather than silently converted into potentially misleading simulations.

Students should be able to actively perform simulated procedures, explore the consequences of their experimental decisions, identify mistakes, and receive constructive feedback aligned with the intended learning objectives.

Ultimately, the research aims to investigate whether such generated and validated laboratory experiences can provide a scientifically reliable complement to physical laboratory education and support students' conceptual understanding and ability to apply knowledge to meaningful problems.

The effectiveness of this approach should be assessed through explicit measures of source fidelity, functional fidelity, and educational learning outcomes rather than assumed from the successful generation or visual appearance of a simulation.

**Research boundary:** The desired outcome defines the intended capabilities and educational benefits to be investigated. It does not claim that LearnAI has already achieved these outcomes, that improved student learning has been demonstrated, or that virtual laboratories can replace all aspects of physical laboratory education.
